## The Three Things That Actually Matter

Over the years I kept reducing entrepreneurship down to fewer and fewer moving parts. I ended up with three.

| Pillar | The question it answers | What happens if it's missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product** | Is this worth paying for? | You build something nobody wants |
| **Marketing & Sales** | Does the right person ever hear about it? | Great product, zero reach |
| **Supply & Demand** | Is there pressure to buy *now*? | Price collapses toward zero |

I learned them in exactly that order — and I learned each one because the previous one wasn't enough.

## Chapter 1 — I Thought Product Was Everything

My first obsession was the **Business Model Canvas** (Strategyzer). I lived in two blocks of it:

- **Value Proposition** — what pain am I killing?
- **Customer Segment** — whose pain is it?

```text
        ┌──────────────────────┐   ┌──────────────────────┐
        │  VALUE PROPOSITION   │ → │  CUSTOMER SEGMENT    │
        │  pain relievers      │   │  jobs, pains, gains  │
        │  gain creators       │ ← │                      │
        └──────────────────────┘   └──────────────────────┘
                    ↑
              I lived here.
              The other 7 blocks were... decorations.
```

## Chapter 2 — So I Learned Marketing and Sales

The obvious fix: if people don't know, go tell them. I went deep on **funnels**.

```text
   Awareness   ██████████████████████   1000
   Interest    ████████████              420
   Desire      ████                      110
   Action      █                          12
```

| What I fixed | What I expected | What I got |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing pages | Traffic → signups | Traffic, few signups |
| Ad / outreach copy | Attention → interest | Attention, no urgency |
| Follow-up sequences | Interest → purchase | Polite "maybe later" |

_Write about: the specific funnel you built, the channel you used, and the number that disappointed you most._

**The lie I believed:** _"A funnel creates customers."_

**The truth:** a funnel doesn't create demand — it only **routes** demand that already exists. I had built a very clean pipe with nothing flowing through it.

## Chapter 3 — The Missing Piece: Demand and Supply

This is the part nobody told me.

> It is not enough to have a desirable product. You need to **generate systematic demand** for it. If demand is 0, the price is 0.

Desirability is an opinion. Demand is a behavior. You can be admired and still be broke.

```text
   PRICE = f(Demand, Scarcity)

   Demand = 0  →  Price = 0     (no matter how good the product is)
   Demand > 0, Supply → ∞  →  Price → cost
   Demand > 0, Supply limited  →  Price → value
```

| Stage | What I optimized | What I was missing | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Product / value proposition | Distribution | Unseen |
| 2 | Funnels / sales | Demand generation | Unbought |
| 3 | Demand & supply | — | _You fill this in_ |

_Write about: what "systematic demand" concretely looks like for you now — a repeatable trigger, a positioning shift, a scarcity mechanic, a category you create rather than compete in._

## Putting It Back Together

The three pillars aren't a checklist. They're multiplication:

```text
   Value  ×  Reach  ×  Demand  =  Revenue

   9  ×  0  ×  0  =  0     ← me, chapter 1
   9  ×  7  ×  0  =  0     ← me, chapter 2
   9  ×  7  ×  ?  =  ?     ← me, now
```

Any zero zeroes the whole thing. That's why "my product is great" was never an argument.
